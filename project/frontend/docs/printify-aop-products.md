## 1. What is All-Over-Print (AOP)?

All-Over-Print (AOP) refers to a printing technique where the design covers the **entire surface** of a garment or product — front, back, sides, sleeves, and seams — rather than being confined to a specific print area (chest, back panel, etc.).

Key characteristics:
- Design extends edge-to-edge and seam-to-seam
- No visible boundaries between printed and unprinted areas
- Supports photorealistic images, complex patterns, and gradients
- The fabric itself becomes the design canvas

AOP products are produced using **sublimation printing** (dye-sublimation), which permanently bonds the ink into the material at the molecular level.

---

## 2. How AOP Printing Works (Sublimation)

### The Sublimation Process

1. **Design is digitally printed** onto special transfer paper using sublimation inks
2. **Transfer paper is placed** on the blank product
3. **Heat press applies intense heat and pressure** (typically 375–400°F / 190–205°C for 30–60 seconds)
4. **Ink sublimates** — transforms from solid directly to gas without passing through liquid state
5. **Gas penetrates** the polyester fibers or polymer coating of the product
6. **On cooling**, the fibers close and permanently trap the dye inside

### Result
- Print is part of the material, not a layer on top of it
- Smooth to the touch — completely unnoticeable as a printed surface
- Seam-to-seam coverage with no white borders
- Permanent, fade-resistant, crack-resistant

## 3. AOP vs Other Print Methods

| Feature | AOP (Sublimation) | DTG (Direct-to-Garment) | Screen Printing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coverage | Full garment (edge-to-edge) | Specific print area only | Specific print area only |
| Color range | Unlimited (photorealistic) | Full color | Limited (spot colors, typically 1–8) |
| White ink | Not possible | Yes, requires underbase | Yes |
| Dark fabrics | Not possible | Yes (with underbase) | Yes |
| Material | Polyester/polymer-coated only | Cotton/blends | Cotton/blends |
| Feel | Part of fabric (no texture) | Slight raised texture | Raised texture |
| Minimum order | No minimum | No minimum | High minimums (setup cost) |
| Per-unit cost | Higher than screen printing | Moderate | Low at scale |
| Gradient support | Excellent | Good | Poor (halftone only) |
| Durability | Permanent (won't crack or peel) | Good (may fade slightly) | Good (may crack) |
| Best for | Patterns, photos, full designs | Complex logos on dark shirts | Simple designs, bulk orders |

## 4. Material Requirements

### Mandatory: Polyester or Polymer-Coated Substrates

Sublimation ink **only bonds** with polyester fibers or polymer-coated surfaces. The dye chemically bonds with the synthetic polymer chains.

| Material | AOP Compatibility |
|---|---|
| 100% polyester | Excellent — full vibrancy |
| Polyester/spandex blend | Excellent — stretchy AOP items (leggings, swimwear) |
| Polyester/nylon blend | Good |
| Poly/cotton blend (>50% poly) | Reduced vibrancy — colors appear washed out |
| 100% cotton | Not compatible — ink will not bond |
| Dark colored fabric | Not compatible — sublimation cannot produce opaque white |
| White/light colored base | Required — sublimation is transparent, base color shows through |

### Why White/Light Base is Mandatory
Sublimation inks are transparent dyes — they tint the fabric rather than coat it. A white polyester base gives the fullest, most accurate color reproduction. Any base color will shift the printed colors:
- White base → accurate colors
- Light grey base → slightly muted/cool tones
- Colored base → design colors blend with base color

## 5. Design File Requirements

### Accepted File Formats
- **PNG** (recommended — supports transparency)
- **JPEG** (acceptable — no transparency support)

### Resolution
| Product Size | Minimum DPI | Recommended DPI |
|---|---|---|
| Small items (socks, accessories) | 150 DPI | 300 DPI |
| Standard apparel (S–XL) | 150–200 DPI | 300 DPI |
| Large apparel (2XL–4XL) or home décor | 120–150 DPI | 200–300 DPI |
| Desk mats, large blankets | 120 DPI | 150–200 DPI |

### Color Profile
- **Upload files in RGB color space** (not CMYK)
- Printify's system converts RGB → CMYK for production, but starting in CMYK may cause unexpected color shifts
- CMYK files may result in color variations vs what you see on screen
- Colors on screen (backlit RGB monitor) will always look slightly more vivid than the printed result

### Bleed Area Coverage
- **Fill 100% of the bleed area** in your design file
- The bleed area (marked with dashed lines in Printify's editor) represents the trim zone during cut-and-sew
- Do NOT place key design elements, text, or logos in the bleed zone — they may be cut off
- Leave important content 0.5–1 inch (1.3–2.5 cm) inside the bleed boundary

### Template Files
Each AOP product in Printify's catalog includes a downloadable design template (PNG/PSD) showing:
- Safe zone (where design elements should stay)
- Bleed zone (where trim may occur)
- Seam lines
- Panel layout (front, back, sleeves, hood sections)

## 6. Canvas Dimensions and Bleed Zones

AOP products use a **panel-based layout**. The design file covers the entire garment laid flat, including all panels that will be cut and sewn together. Below are typical canvas dimensions for common AOP product types.

> Note: Exact dimensions vary by blueprint and print provider. Always download the template from Printify's Product Creator for the specific SKU.

### AOP T-Shirts

| Size (garment) | Typical Canvas Width | Typical Canvas Height | Bleed |
|---|---|---|---|
| XS–S | ~5200 px | ~3800 px | 0.5 inch per edge |
| M–L | ~5600 px | ~4200 px | 0.5 inch per edge |
| XL–2XL | ~6000 px | ~4600 px | 0.5 inch per edge |
| 3XL–4XL | ~6400 px | ~5000 px | 0.5 inch per edge |

Panels included: Front body, Back body, Left sleeve, Right sleeve

### AOP Hoodies (Pullover)

| Size (garment) | Typical Canvas Width | Typical Canvas Height | Bleed |
|---|---|---|---|
| XS–S | ~5800 px | ~4600 px | 0.5–0.75 inch |
| M–L | ~6200 px | ~5000 px | 0.5–0.75 inch |
| XL–2XL | ~6600 px | ~5400 px | 0.5–0.75 inch |

Panels included: Front body, Back body, Left sleeve, Right sleeve, Hood (outer), Pocket panel (if applicable)

### AOP Zip Hoodies

Zip hoodies have a split front panel. The zipper runs down the center, meaning the front is two separate panels:
- Left front panel
- Right front panel
- Back panel
- Sleeves
- Hood

Canvas dimensions similar to pullover hoodies. The center seam/zipper means designs that cross the front must account for the zip interruption.

### AOP Leggings

| Coverage | Typical Canvas Dimensions |
|---|---|
| Full leg (waistband to ankle) | ~4000 × 6000 px @ 150 DPI |
| Panels | Front leg + Back leg (usually a single wrap-around template) |

Bleed: 0.5 inch per edge. Waistband area is typically a separate elastic band — design should account for waistband coverage.

### AOP Socks

Sock templates are wrapped designs covering the full 360° of the sock surface. Typically:
- Canvas wraps around the sock cylinder
- Design repeats or wraps seamlessly
- Bleed on all edges

### AOP Swimwear / Shorts

Bikini tops, swimsuit bottoms, board shorts:
- Templates show separate panels (front, back, straps)
- Stretch factor must be considered — designs may compress/expand when worn

### AOP Tote Bags

| Bag Size | Canvas Dimensions | Panels |
|---|---|---|
| Small | ~3600 × 4200 px | Front face, Back face (sometimes connected) |
| Medium | ~4200 × 5000 px | Front face, Back face |
| Large | ~5000 × 6000 px | Front face, Back face |

Material: 100% polyester (weather-resistant) with cotton straps. The cotton straps cannot be sublimation-printed.

### General Bleed Zone Rules

- **Minimum bleed**: 0.5 inch (1.27 cm) on all edges
- **Recommended bleed**: 0.75 inch (1.9 cm) for seam areas
- **Safe zone for key content**: Keep text, logos, and focal design elements at least 0.5 inch inside the trim line
- **Seam areas**: Designs crossing seam lines will show a slight discontinuity due to cut-and-sew — this is normal and expected
- **Pattern designs**: Seamless/repeating patterns handle seams best (no visible interruption)

## 9. Design Best Practices

### Designs That Work Best with AOP

**Pattern and Repeat Designs**
- Seamless repeating patterns are ideal — seam discontinuities are invisible
- Geometric patterns, florals, abstract repeats
- The pattern can continue across seams naturally

**Photorealistic and Gradient-Rich Designs**
- AOP/sublimation excels at photorealistic images (landscapes, space, underwater scenes)
- Smooth gradients render beautifully without banding
- Complex color blends that would be impossible with screen printing

**Full-Coverage Abstract Art**
- Painterly textures, watercolor washes, splatter effects
- The full-garment canvas allows designs that would be too complex for a chest-print area

**Bold Graphic Designs**
- Large typography or graphics that use the whole garment as canvas
- Character/mascot designs that wrap around the body

### Design Considerations Specific to AOP

**Seam Awareness**
- Seams run along shoulders, sides, and sleeves
- Designs crossing seams will have a slight shift (1–3mm typical)
- Use this intentionally or design around it with patterns that flow naturally

**Zipper Area (Zip Hoodies)**
- The zipper splits the front design down the center
- Design must account for the zipper covering the center line
- Avoid placing text or logos centered on the front of zip hoodies — they will be split

**Hood Design**
- The hood has a separate panel
- Hood design must be created for the hood template specifically
- Designs do not automatically flow from body to hood

**Sleeve Direction**
- Sleeve templates show the sleeve laid flat
- The design will wrap around the arm when worn
- Account for cylindrical distortion — wide designs will appear narrower on the arm

**Size Scaling**
- AOP templates are size-specific (different canvas per garment size)
- Creating one design file for all sizes may result in different proportions at different sizes
- Best practice: create a flexible/scalable design or use Printify's size-scaling feature

**Avoiding Key Elements at Edges**
- Never place text, faces, logos, or focal points within the bleed zone
- Maintain a 0.5–1 inch safe margin inside the bleed line
- Side seam areas may compress slightly — avoid thin details near side edges

### File Preparation Workflow

1. Download the product template from Printify's Product Creator (PNG/PSD)
2. Create design in Photoshop, Illustrator, Canva, or similar at 300 DPI
3. Work in RGB color space throughout
4. Position all key design elements within the safe zone
5. Fill bleed area completely (no white gaps at edges)
6. Export as PNG (preferred) or JPEG
7. Upload to Printify Product Creator
8. Review mockup carefully — check seam areas and panel alignment
9. Order a sample before publishing for sale

## 10. Color Management

### RGB vs CMYK

| Aspect | RGB (Required) | CMYK (Avoid) |
|---|---|---|
| Color space | Additive (light) | Subtractive (ink) |
| On-screen appearance | Matches monitor | May look dull on screen |
| After upload conversion | Converted to CMYK by Printify | Double conversion = color shift |
| Recommendation | Always upload in RGB | Avoid uploading CMYK files |

### Color Vibrancy Expectations
- Colors on a backlit monitor (RGB) always look more vivid than printed output
- Printed colors are CMYK (subtractive) — naturally less saturated than screen
- Neon/fluorescent colors cannot be reproduced in CMYK — they will print as standard equivalents
- Always order a physical sample to verify color before mass selling

### White in AOP Designs
- Sublimation cannot produce white ink — it is the absence of dye
- White areas in your design = the base white of the fabric
- Ensure the blank product is truly white (not off-white or cream) for accurate white reproduction
- Pure white fabric gives best contrast for color vibrancy

### Safe Color Zones for AOP
- Bright, saturated colors in RGB render well
- Avoid pure black (#000000) — may appear very dark but not always true black
- Pastels may appear slightly washed out — boost saturation in design if targeting bright pastels
- Dark colors render well on white polyester (dark blue, forest green, burgundy, black)

## 11. Common Pitfalls and Limitations

### Technical Limitations

| Limitation | Details |
|---|---|
| No white ink | Cannot print opaque white — white = blank fabric |
| No dark fabrics | Sublimation only on white/light base; dark fabric = invisible design |
| Polyester only | 100% cotton or high-cotton blends will not hold sublimation ink |
| Seam discontinuity | Designs crossing seams will show a slight shift — unavoidable |
| Color shift | CMYK conversion from RGB; always order samples to verify |
| Size-specific templates | Each garment size needs appropriate canvas scaling |
| No texture/hand-feel | Unlike screen printing or DTG — design is completely smooth |

### Design Mistakes to Avoid

- **Leaving white gaps at edges**: The product must have design coverage in the bleed zone — white gaps at edges look unfinished
- **Ignoring the hood template**: Many designs look great on the body but the hood is plain — design both or use a complementary solid color
- **Text in the bleed zone**: Text at the very edge will be cut off during trimming
- **High-resolution photos at low DPI**: A 72 DPI photo upscaled to fill the canvas will appear pixelated on the final product
- **CMYK file upload**: Will cause color double-conversion and unexpected color variations
- **Centered design on zip hoodie front**: The zipper will split any centered front design
- **Not ordering a sample**: Screen mockups do not perfectly represent seam behavior, color output, or fabric texture

### Production Realities

- **Seam variation**: Cut-and-sew tolerance is typically ±0.5–1 cm — expect slight variation in where seam lines fall
- **Hood vs body color matching**: If the hood uses different panel templates, there may be a slight color temperature difference between panels from different material rolls
- **Wash and care**: Sublimation prints are permanent and durable, but heat and harsh detergents can degrade over time. Recommend cold water wash, no bleach, no tumble dry high heat
- **White creasing**: Areas of the garment that fold/crease during pressing may show slight white marks — inherent to the sublimation process, not a defect in most cases

## 12. Production and Fulfillment

### Print Providers for AOP on Printify

Printify works with multiple print providers for AOP products. Provider selection affects:
- Print quality and color accuracy
- Production time
- Shipping speed and cost
- Geographic fulfillment location

For EU-based fulfillment, provider availability for AOP is more limited than for DTG (see Section 13).

### Typical Production Times
- AOP products generally take **3–7 business days** in production (longer than DTG due to cut-and-sew process)
- Allow additional time for:
  - First-time orders (no production cache)
  - High-demand seasons (Q4, holidays)
  - New design uploads

### Order Fulfillment Flow (Printify)
1. Customer places order in your store
2. Order automatically sent to Printify
3. Printify routes to assigned print provider
4. Provider prints design via sublimation, cuts panels, sews garment
5. Quality check
6. Garment packaged and shipped to customer

### Sample Orders
- Always order a physical sample before publishing AOP products for sale
- Samples cost the base price + shipping
- Use samples to verify: color accuracy, seam alignment, sizing, print sharpness
- Printify offers sample discounts for select providers

## 14. FAQ

**Q: Can I use AOP on dark-colored hoodies or t-shirts?**
A: No. Sublimation/AOP printing requires a white or light-colored polyester base. The dye is transparent — it cannot cover dark base colors.

**Q: Can I print white elements in my AOP design?**
A: "White" in your design file will show as the base fabric color (white polyester). There is no white sublimation ink. This is actually advantageous — white details look clean and crisp on a white base.

**Q: My design looks great on screen but the printed colors look different. Why?**
A: Screen displays use RGB (additive light) which appears more vivid than physical CMYK printed ink. Additionally, sublimation uses dye that bonds into the fiber — colors depend on the base fabric's brightness and the conversion from your RGB file to CMYK print. Always order a sample to calibrate expectations.

**Q: Can I mix AOP with embroidery or DTG on the same product?**
A: Generally no — these are separate production techniques requiring different blank products. Some specialty products (e.g., a sublimation hoodie with an embroidered patch) exist but are not standard in the Printify catalog.

**Q: Does the design look the same on all sizes?**
A: Each garment size has a different canvas dimension. Printify scales designs automatically, but proportions may shift slightly across sizes. For best results, create your design with the mid-range size template (M or L) as the base.

**Q: Can I put text on an AOP product?**
A: Yes, but be careful: text in the bleed zone will be cut off, and text near seams may be disrupted. Keep all text in the safe zone (0.5–1 inch inside the bleed boundary). On zip hoodies, avoid centered front text due to the zipper split.

**Q: How durable is AOP/sublimation printing?**
A: Sublimation is one of the most durable print methods. The dye is embedded in the fiber — it cannot crack, peel, or fade with normal wash and wear. Avoid bleach, high heat drying, and ironing directly on the print to maximize longevity.

**Q: What DPI should my AOP design file be?**
A: 300 DPI is the recommended standard. For very large items (blankets, large desk mats), 120–150 DPI is acceptable. Below 120 DPI will result in visible pixelation on the final product.

**Q: What file format should I upload?**
A: PNG is preferred (supports transparency). JPEG is accepted but does not support transparent areas. Always export in RGB color space.

**Q: The product template shows dashed lines. What do they mean?**
A: Dashed lines in Printify's Product Creator indicate the **bleed zone boundary** — the area that will be trimmed or sewn into seams during production. Fill the entire bleed zone with your design, but keep all important elements (text, logos, faces) inside the safe zone boundary.

**Q: Can I sell AOP products to EU customers?**
A: Yes, but verify your specific blueprint has an EU-based print provider available. Many AOP products on Printify only have non-EU providers, which means longer shipping times to EU customers and potential customs/duty implications.
